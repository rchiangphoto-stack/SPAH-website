What Is RHDV2?

RHDV2 stands for Rabbit Hemorrhagic Disease Virus, type 2 — a highly contagious calicivirus that affects both pet and wild rabbits. It's one of the most serious threats a rabbit can face: it spreads easily, resists the environment, and is fatal in a large share of the rabbits it infects. Many owners only learn the virus exists after it's already too late.

Part of what makes RHDV2 so dangerous is how quietly it moves. Rabbits can die suddenly with little or no warning, and the virus can spread without direct rabbit-to-rabbit contact — it travels on contaminated hay and food, on shoes and clothing, and even on insects. That's why "my rabbit lives indoors" is not protection on its own.

Since being confirmed in domestic and wild rabbits in California, RHDV2 has changed the standard of care for pet rabbits in our area. Vaccination is now widely recommended for essentially every pet rabbit in the state — not just those that go outdoors.

How the RHDV2 Vaccine Works

The RHDV2 vaccine is a USDA-authorized vaccine developed specifically for this virus. It's given as a small injection during a regular rabbit visit — no sedation needed — and most rabbits tolerate it well, with at most some brief tiredness or mild soreness at the injection site.

Protection is built with an initial series followed by a yearly booster. In practice, that usually means a first dose, a booster about three weeks later, and then an annual booster to keep immunity strong. Because the exact timing can depend on your rabbit's age and history, we'll confirm the right schedule when you come in. Healthy rabbits can generally start the vaccine from a young age — if you have a new baby bunny, call us and we'll tell you when to begin.

We pair the vaccine with a quick wellness check, because a vaccine visit is also a good moment to look at teeth, weight, and gut health — the things that quietly cause the most trouble in rabbits. If anything looks off, you'll hear about it then rather than later.

RHDV2 Vaccine FAQ

Is RHDV2 really in California?

Yes. RHDV2 has been confirmed in wild and domestic rabbits in California, which is exactly why vaccination went from "optional" to widely recommended for pet rabbits here. It is not a hypothetical risk in our area.

Does my indoor-only rabbit need the RHDV2 vaccine?

Yes. The virus can be carried in on your shoes and clothing, on hay and greens, and by insects, and it survives in the environment for a long time. Indoor rabbits are still at real risk, so the vaccine is recommended for them too.

How much does the RHDV2 vaccine cost?

We publish our fees on our pricing page so you can plan before you come in. A vaccine visit also includes a brief wellness check. Call (626) 441-1314 and we'll walk you through the cost.

What is the RHDV2 vaccine schedule?

Most rabbits get an initial dose, a booster about three weeks later, and then an annual booster to maintain protection. The exact timing can depend on your rabbit's age and history, so we'll confirm the right plan at your first visit.

Are there side effects?

Most rabbits do very well. The most common reactions are mild and short-lived — a little tiredness or some soreness at the injection site. Serious reactions are uncommon, and we'll go over what to watch for before you leave.

Do I need to be an existing client, or can I just come in?

You don't need a referral or an existing relationship with us — new rabbit patients are welcome. Call (626) 441-1314 or book online, and just mention it's for an RHDV2 vaccine.

Do you offer the RHDV2 vaccine to rabbit rescues?

Yes — we work with local rabbit rescues and are happy to talk about vaccinating rescue rabbits. If you run or foster for a rescue, get in touch and we'll find a way to help.
